Casa 02 design target: 20 × 39 ftAbout 1,360 sf on two floors, plus the carport bay under the upper floor. At this depth every winning layout passes with room for a terrace per home. L1-T1 sets the limit; at 40 ft it is tight, with 91 sf of paving left per home. A 3-bed, 2.5-bath plan in that size is a compact townhouse; a 2-bed + flex plan is the fallback.
Casita design target: 20 × 32 ft640 sf, one story. It must work with and without its own parking, and with its entry from the side yard, the street or an alley.
Confirm with the cityHow ribbon drives, pervious pavers, gravel and decks count toward impervious cover; the small-lot setbacks, coverage and size cap; and that the rear setback is 5 ft next to homes, as assumed here.

Lessons

  • Floor area, not footprint, caps the 50-ft duo: two Casa 02 hit the 3,437 sf cap at 47 ft deep.
  • Casa 01 + Casita fails on a 65-ft lot: Casa 01's roof covers 2,996 sf, so the pair needs 3,636 sf of the 3,250 sf footprint cap. Casa 01 stays a single-family design.
  • Drives decide trios. A drive to the back home fails the impervious cap on the 50-ft and the deep lot (L1-T2, L5-T2); a drive that stops at the middle home passes (L4-T1, L5-T1).
  • Three Casa 02 fit on a corner (L3-T2), with the third facing the side street, but not in a row on a 65-ft lot (L4-T2): a row of three 20-ft homes needs a 70-ft lot.
  • A 50-ft lot splits into two small lots that each take a Casa 02 (L6-S2), but not three (L6-S3): 16.7-ft lots are too narrow.
  • Parking under the upper floor costs each Casa 02 200 sf of floor area but adds no footprint or paving. An open pad instead would add 200 sf of paving per home, which the trios cannot spare.
